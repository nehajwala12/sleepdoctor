Section 01 · Overview — the business answer

Sleep Media is a $10.7M-a-year cash business worth more managed and harvested than sold today.

This packet looks at OneCare Media as a business: what Sleep Media earns and how to extract its value, what has improved this year in search, AI and revenue, what Sleep Care has to prove before it gets more capital, and what each path is worth. Lender and capital-structure points are kept to a short appendix (tab 10); the company's 13-week cash forecast is read on the Cash tab (tab 09).

A managed harvest of Sleep Media is worth more than selling it today.

Run on its own, Sleep Media earns about $10.7M a year: $16.3M of 2026 revenue less $3.0M of direct cost and about $2.6M of corporate cost for a stand-alone company. Harvested and actively managed, that cash is worth about $45M in the base case, against about $29M for a managed sale today after fees and timing ($32M to $37M gross).

The gap is the value of running the asset well rather than selling it into a soft market. About $13M of it comes from ten commercial levers (CPC floors, AI referrals and licensing, pharma advertising, email, rebuilding the clusters that lost rank, a lean cost base). If this year's gains in rank and monetisation hold, the managed harvest is worth about $63M, close to management's own plan.

What holds. The asset generates cash in every modelled case for five years, because its cost base is small and partly variable; even the severe case returns cash every year.

What to watch. If Google traffic falls at the downside pace, the harvest is worth about $29M, roughly what a sale fetches today; the trigger to sell is a further step-down in commercial-page clicks after the holiday quarter.

Rankings, AI citations and monetisation improved this year, though traffic is still falling.

The commercial pages rebuilt in the second quarter climbed to about third on pillows, toppers and the head term 'best mattress'; '/best-pillows' is now the site's most-clicked page. ChatGPT cites sleepfoundation.org in 4.4% of commercial answers in a 107,000-answer study, more than any other domain in its top 20, and AI-referral revenue rose about fivefold between June and August.

Against that, Google clicks to the site fell 48% year on year in October and are still falling week by week, the CPAP, back-pain and side-sleeper mattress pages lost their rankings, and the gains are concentrated in pillows, which earn about a ninth as much per click as mattresses.

What holds. Monetisation is the strongest gain: between August 2025 and August 2026 click-through to merchants rose from 42% to 53% and revenue per click from $6.42 to $7.02, so third-quarter revenue fell 9% while Google clicks fell 39% (16% on July and August actuals; September is the plan's estimate).

What to watch. Average position in Search Console looks much better than a year ago, but most of that is a reporting change in September 2025; query-level positions and clicks are the read that matters.

Sources: Google Search Console (query and page cuts, 4 Nov 2026); RankSpot most-cited sources (1 Sep–2 Nov 2026); LRP Revenue-SM Organic; Q3-26 board preread.

On its own, Sleep Media earns about twice what management reports.

Management's segment reporting charges Sleep Media with the whole $7.4M corporate layer, so it shows $5.6M of EBITDA for 2026. A stand-alone media company of 25 people needs about $2.6M of corporate cost, which leaves $10.7M; the other $4.8M is the corporate cost of running Sleep Care, and it stays with Sleep Care after any separation.

The holiday quarter is a third of the year's cash: the plan has $4.3M of Media EBITDA before corporate cost in October to December, helped by a guaranteed $16–20 cost per click from the largest mattress partner on 15 pages until 31 December.

What holds. The people who produce the revenue already sit in the media unit; nothing in the direct cost base has to change for it to run on its own.

What to watch. October Google clicks to the commercial pages fell 15% from September while the plan has October search revenue up 3%; at September's revenue per click that is about $240K short for the month.

Sources: LRP Financials and Revenue-SM Organic; Search Console via Markifact; Q3-26 board preread pp.13–16.

Insurance demand is real, though Sleep Care has yet to convert it profitably.

Moving to insurance tripled patient interest at about 40% of the cost per action, and insurance now supplies about 40% of new patients. But in July only 5.3% of insurance form views became a shipped test, and four-month gross profit per patient has stayed below acquisition cost in every 2026 quarter on the fulfilled basis management now reports.

On its own plan Sleep Care needs about $17M of cash in 2027 once its stranded corporate cost, insurance receivables and capex are counted. Media's managed cash covers about two thirds of that; the rest needs outside money.

What holds. On management's sold basis cash-pay cohorts were profitable early in 2026, gross margin rose seven points in a year, and weekly acquisition cost fell back to $124–184 in October once brand spend was cut.

What to watch. The December throughput read (target 16.5%) and the March unit-economics read decide whether the 2027 plan is possible; both are the plan's own milestones.

Sources: Q3-26 board preread pp.22–35; Oct-19-26 company update pp.11–23; Sleep Care dashboard 1 Nov 2026; CreditSwan model.

In the base case, gated Build-and-Harvest is worth the most on either odds.

Seven paths were valued as businesses, before any debt. Harvesting Media while funding Sleep Care tranche by tranche against its own milestones is worth about $52M on evidence-based odds and $71M on management-implied odds. Harvesting Media and selling Sleep Care now is close behind on evidence odds at $50M.

The management plan without gates is worth $43M on evidence odds, because the cash keeps flowing into Sleep Care in the case where it does not work. Selling Media now is worth $36M and a whole-company sale about $33M.

What holds. Gated Build-and-Harvest stays on top in the base and gains-hold cases on both odds sets; the gate, not the funding source, is what separates it from the management plan.

What to watch. In the severe case selling Media now is the best path; the sale option should stay ready rather than be exercised today.

Sources: CreditSwan model (Value tab); assumptions A-01 to A-36.

The commercial levers add about $13M of value on a risk-weighted basis.

The largest levers are commercial terms and cost: renewing guaranteed cost-per-click terms with the top mattress partners for 2027, running the stand-alone company on a lean corporate layer, spreading the AI-referral rebuild to every commercial category, and selling pharma and endemic advertising around Dr. Breus. AI and test-data licensing is smaller but new.

Each lever is weighted by its odds of success, from 30% for the magazine to 80% for domain sales; delivered in full they would be worth about twice as much.

What holds. Most levers extend things already working this year: the pillow rebuild, the 3Z floor, the AI-referral lift and the advertising run-rate.

What to watch. The 3Z floor ends on 31 December; without new terms, revenue per click on the 15 floor pages falls back by about $4 in January.

Sources: CreditSwan value plan; Q3-26 board preread pp.16–20, 52–61; Oct-19-26 company update.

Three decisions this quarter protect most of the value.

First, protect the holiday quarter and lock 2027 commercial terms before 31 December. Second, put Sleep Care on tranche funding against its own milestones, with marketing scaled only when four-month gross profit per patient exceeds acquisition cost. Third, run Sleep Media as a separate business with its own accounts, team and agreements, so that a sale can happen whenever the evidence calls for one.

None of the three needs a transaction, and each keeps every later option open.

What holds. Management's own plan contains the milestones and the scale rule; the decisions hold the company to them.

What to watch. Q4 marketing for Sleep Care is the fastest way the holiday cash leaves the business; about $2.5M is planned before any gate is met.

Sources: Q3-26 board preread; Oct-19-26 company update; Sleep Care dashboard 1 Nov 2026.

Section 03 · Sleep Media — the commercial engine

Sleep Media earns more per visit than a year ago from a shrinking Google audience.

This tab describes the business that produces the cash: where the revenue comes from, what each category earns per click, how monetisation has changed, who the revenue depends on, the audiences Google does not control, what the business would cost to run on its own and how its revenue could develop. The balanced read of this year's gains and losses is on the next tab.

Sleep Foundation search still earns about four fifths of media revenue.

Sleep Media revenue was $4.97M in Q3 2025 and about $4.5M in Q3 2026. Google-dependent affiliate revenue is still about 80% of the mix, but the top three best-mattress pages fell from 34% to 20% of revenue in a year as mid-tier and long-tail pages grew and advertising, AI referrals, YouTube and email rose from 3% to 20%.

Diversified revenue was $880K in Q3 2026: advertising $358K, YouTube $222K, AI referrals $195K and email $105K. 3Z Brands alone is 44% of direct advertising, so the non-Google line has its own concentration.

What holds. Diversification ran ahead of management's own plan: non-search revenue beat budget by 24% through May and advertising was 18 times its level a year earlier.

What to watch. The 3Z guaranteed cost-per-click agreement ($16–20 a click on 15 pages) ends on 31 December 2026; January revenue per click is the first test of 2027.

Sources: Q3-26 board preread pp.12–20; Q2-26 preread pp.16–18.

Mattress pages earn about nine times more per click than pillow pages.

In Q3 2026 a click out from a best-mattress page earned about $16 against about $1.84 from a pillow page, because a mattress sale carries a far larger commission. Best-mattress pages produced about 64% of Sleep Foundation search revenue on less than a third of the clicks out.

This is why the year's ranking gains matter less than they look: pillow revenue rose about half while mattress revenue fell about a quarter, and the mattress decline was about six times larger in dollars.

What holds. Revenue per click on the mattress pages rose a fifth in a year, helped by the partner floor and by the rebuilt pages sending better-qualified clicks.

What to watch. The mattress category is cyclical and its partners are under pressure: US mattress units fell 13% in 2025 and the largest partner is shifting budget to retail.

Sources: LRP Revenue-SM Organic (category traffic, click-through, clicks and revenue per click); ISPA 2026 report; Q3-26 board preread.

Higher click-through and revenue per click offset most of the traffic loss.

Between 2024 and 2026 commercial traffic fell by about half while revenue per visit rose by more than a quarter: click-through to merchants went from 37% to 47% for the year (about 51% since July) after the page rebuilds and revenue per click held near $6.50, then rose above $8 in the autumn plan on the 3Z floor. Organic revenue therefore fell about a third, not a half.

The plan for 2027 to 2029 keeps click-through at 50% and revenue per click near $6.90 while commercial traffic falls another 11% in 2027 and then holds. Monetisation can keep improving, but not at this year's pace for long.

What holds. The rebuilt pages convert better: the second-quarter changes (answer blocks, plain comparison tables, tester profiles, how-we-test sections) lifted click-through on every commercial page type.

What to watch. If partners cut commission rates as their own volumes fall, revenue per click reverses and the traffic decline shows through in full.

Sources: LRP Revenue-SM Organic and Financials-Sleep Media; Q3-26 board preread pp.15–16.

Concentration is falling, though one partner still carries the holiday quarter.

The largest affiliate partner, 3Z, also bought 44% of third-quarter advertising, and its guaranteed $16–20 cost per click on 15 pages until 31 December is the single largest support for the fourth-quarter plan. Best-mattress pages are about two thirds of search revenue, so a few mattress partners carry most of the affiliate economics.

Advertising was well ahead of budget in the spring on 3Z's first AI-content programme, fell behind in July as the summer budget assumed a ramp, and was level with budget by September; October was close to budget.

What holds. The floor proves pricing power: the test-lab data and the rebuilt pages are worth more per click to partners than an open auction pays.

What to watch. Saatva is also a Comvest Credit Partners borrower, so any renegotiation or partner bid needs the two deal teams kept apart.

Sources: Q3-26 board preread pp.16–20; 3Z Brands releases; Comvest Credit Partners Saatva release (2022).

YouTube and email give Sleep Media an audience Google does not control.

The two YouTube channels have about 594K subscribers, up 51K since June; the Sleep Doctor channel's third-quarter views were 47% above the second quarter and watch hours 78% higher. The email file has 3.26M active subscribers after weekly suppression of inactive addresses, with 431K new sign-ups in the quarter. Dr. Breus had more than 90 press placements between September and March.

These audiences earn little today: email about $0.13 per subscriber a year and YouTube about $0.9M a year. Their value is that they keep earning when Google moves, and that a buyer would pay for them separately.

What holds. ChatGPT cites sleepfoundation.org in about one commercial answer in twenty-three, the highest ChatGPT rate among the 20 most-cited domains in a 107,000-answer study, which is an audience in itself.

What to watch. The audiences depend heavily on Dr. Breus; his agreement and its change-of-control terms decide how much of this value travels in any sale.

Sources: Q3-26 board preread; Q1-26 board deck; RankSpot most-cited sources; Search Console via Markifact.

A stand-alone Sleep Media needs about $2.6M of corporate cost a year.

The media business has 25 direct employees in content, marketing, business development, analytics and technology, costing about $2.5M in payroll and contractors plus $0.5M of tools and other costs. Management's reporting then charges it the whole shared-services layer: $3.8M of payroll for the executive team, finance, people and analytics, $1.4M of professional fees, $0.8M of software and $1.4M of facilities, travel, fees and the accrued management fee.

A stand-alone media company of this size needs a general manager (the current SVP already runs the unit), a finance lead with outsourced accounting, fractional HR and legal, audit, insurance and its own tools: about $2.6M, or $2.0M run lean.

What holds. None of the direct media cost base has to change; the people who produce the revenue are already in the unit.

What to watch. Whoever owns Sleep Care after a separation inherits about $4.8M of corporate cost that today sits in the media line.

Sources: LRP Financials-Shared Serv, Financials-SM Less SS, Labor and Headcount.

How far the search decline runs decides most of the value.

Revenue is modelled in three streams from the 2026 forecast: Google search affiliate revenue ($13.8M), AI-referral affiliate revenue ($0.5M, running at about $1M a year by September) and non-search revenue ($2.0M, planned at about $2.7M a year in the fourth quarter). The base case takes 15% a year off search revenue for two years, then 10–12%, holds AI referrals near $0.9M and non-search near $2.4M.

The gains-hold case assumes this year's monetisation and ranking gains slow the search decline to 5–8% a year; the downside and severe cases run it at 15–35%. Management's plan grows total revenue to $17.5M.

What holds. Even the downside case produces positive cash for five years, because the cost base is small and partly variable.

What to watch. The base case is milder than the October click trend (−48% year on year) because revenue has been falling less than half as fast as clicks (−16% against −39% on July and August actuals); if that gap closes, the downside case applies.

Sources: CreditSwan model; LRP; Search Console via Markifact.

Monthly and channel data show where media revenue and cash come from.

Secondary exhibits: the seasonality of media cash, the long-run traffic series, quarterly non-search revenue, the scorecards management runs the unit on and the initiative list.

What holds. The scorecards show the same pattern every month this year: traffic below budget, click-through and revenue per click above it.

What to watch. In 2025 the fourth quarter delivered 18% of media cash after the December core update; the 2026 plan needs 33%. Figures from September 2026 onward are the plan.

Section 04 · Momentum — rank, AI and revenue

Rank, AI visibility and monetisation have improved this year, while Google traffic keeps falling.

This tab weighs what has gone right for Sleep Media in 2026 against what has gone wrong, using Search Console, the company's own revenue build and three outside studies of AI answers. Each section states the gain, the offsetting loss and how durable the gain looks.

This year's gains are real, though they slow the decline rather than reverse it.

Five things improved: the rebuilt commercial pages climbed in Google, AI engines cite the site heavily, AI-referral revenue more than quadrupled after the rebuild, click-through and revenue per click rose, and non-Google revenue went from 3% to 20% of the mix. Together they explain why third-quarter media revenue fell 9% while Google clicks fell 39%; on July and August actuals alone the fall was 16%, because September is the plan's estimate.

Three things got worse: Google clicks keep falling week by week, the high-value long-tail mattress and CPAP pages lost their rankings, and the gains sit in categories that earn less per click. The improvements buy time and lift the cash; they do not yet show a floor under the traffic.

What holds. The click-through and diversification gains are within management's control and have held since the spring; revenue per click passed last year's level only in August.

What to watch. The ranking gains are the least durable: the same pages moved several positions after each Google update in the last two years.

Sources: Search Console (4 Nov 2026); LRP Revenue-SM Organic; Q3-26 board preread; RankSpot (1 Sep–2 Nov 2026); LLM Authority Index (Jul–Oct 2026).

Pillow and topper pages climbed close to the top of Google's results.

The second-quarter rebuild of every non-mattress 'best' page (answer blocks at the top, plain comparison tables, tester profiles, proprietary test scores) moved 'best pillows' from about tenth in Google to about third or fourth and 'best pillow' from about twelfth to about third. 'Best mattress topper' reached second in June. The head term 'best mattress' recovered to about third through most of 2026 after falling to tenth last winter, and 'best mattresses 2026' has ranked first or second in most months.

The losses are on high-value mattress and sleep-apnea terms: 'best CPAP machine' fell from first to fifth, 'best mattress for back pain' from third to sixth and 'best mattress for side sleepers' from third to eighth, with clicks down 85% to 92%.

What holds. Better ranking still earns fewer clicks than it used to: 'best mattress' ranked higher in August 2026 than a year earlier yet drew 23% fewer clicks, and the topper page lost 27% of its clicks year on year, because AI answers and forums sit above the results.

What to watch. By October some gains had slipped ('best mattress topper' from 1.7 in June to 3.4, 'pillow for side sleepers' to 4.5) and CPAP and back pain fell further (7.1 and 8.8). The May 2026 core update is the nearest precedent for the holiday updates: June clicks on 'best mattress' fell from 2,627 to 210 before recovering, and Sistrix listed the site among the update's US losers.

Sources: Google Search Console via Markifact (monthly query cuts, Jul-25 to Oct-26); Q3-26 board preread p.15.

The best-pillows page became the site's most-clicked page as mattress pages fell.

Across August to October, '/best-pillows' drew 82K Google clicks, up 53K on a year earlier and more than the sleep-calculator and melatonin-dosage pages, which led the site a year earlier. Body pillows, cooling pillows, mattress-in-a-box, king and Canada mattress pages also gained.

'/best-mattress' drew 50K clicks, 39K fewer than a year earlier, and the back-pain, side-sleeper, anti-snoring and CPAP pages lost a combined 110K. Against May to July the two largest pages grew ('/best-mattress' up 27%, '/best-pillows' up 18%), but clicks to the commercial pages as a whole fell 22%, the size of the sale-review trigger; the trigger counts a further fall from the October level.

What holds. The recovery on the two largest commercial pages since the summer is the most encouraging single signal in the data.

What to watch. The gaining pages are mostly pillows and bedding, where a click earns about $2; the losing pages include mattress and CPAP terms where it earns many times that.

Sources: Google Search Console via Markifact (page cuts, 4 Nov 2026).

AI engines cite Sleep Foundation heavily, and AI-referral revenue is growing from a small base.

In a study of 107,000 AI answers to commercial prompts between September and November 2026, sleepfoundation.org was the eighth most-cited domain overall and the only health or sleep domain in the top twenty. ChatGPT cited it in 4.4% of answers, more than any other domain in that top twenty; Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode cited it in under 1%.

A smaller monthly index of mattress-buying answers across six AI engines puts Sleep Foundation's presence at 90% in October, against 44% to 60% for Sleepopolis, Mattress Clarity and Mattress Nerd, all owned by Pillar4. Its share of actual recommendations is volatile: 7.9% in July, 2.0% in September and 4.0% in October.

What holds. AI visibility is an asset a buyer would value and a licensing counterparty would pay for; it rests on the test lab and the medical-review process, which competitors find hard to copy.

What to watch. The visibility is concentrated in ChatGPT; Google's AI surfaces, which sit above the search results that pay the bills, cite the site far less.

AI-referral revenue grew fast after the rebuild, though it is still small. AI visits to sleepfoundation.org were roughly flat at 25K to 41K a month in 2026; what changed in July was where they landed. After the commercial-page rebuild, 62% to 66% of AI visits landed on commercial pages against 21% to 36% in the first half, and revenue per AI visit rose from $0.35 in January to $1.68 in August.

AI-referral revenue was $66K in August and about $88K was pacing in September, about 7% of September's planned search revenue. ChatGPT supplies about 96% of identifiable AI visits.

What holds. One 94-brand study found ChatGPT referrals converting at 1.81% against 1.39% for non-branded search, and a 973-site study found ChatGPT conversion improving month by month.

What to watch. A larger study of 973 retailers found affiliate links converting 86% more often than ChatGPT referrals; management's own rule is to treat AI revenue as upside until it holds for a full quarter.

Sources: RankSpot most-cited sources (1 Sep–2 Nov 2026); LLM Authority Index mattress category (Jul–Oct 2026); LLM Pulse health citations (Apr–Jul 2026); Q3-26 board preread pp.16–18; Kaiser and Schulze, Marketing Science (2026); Visibility Labs (2026).

Click-through to merchants rose about ten points as the pages were rebuilt.

Click-through from the commercial pages to merchants ran 31% to 42% through 2025 and 47% to 53% from May 2026. Section 05 · Sleep Care — what the build has to prove

Sleep Care has proven insurance demand and has yet to convert it at a profit.
