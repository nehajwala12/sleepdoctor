Section 01 · Overview — the answer for the Comvest call

Sleep Media is the only cash engine and it funds a plan that keeps missing.

Comvest asked whether Sleep Media can be carved out of Sleep Care and its cash harvested. The short answer is yes, in substance within weeks and in legal form within months, and the harvest is worth about $29M to $68M in present value depending on how fast Google traffic keeps falling. The harder finding is that under the management ask every dollar of that cash is spent on Sleep Care through 2028 while the senior claim compounds from $103M to $140M.

Carving out Sleep Media is viable and worth tens of millions to the senior lenders.

Sleep Media (sleepfoundation.org, sleepapnea.org, the media side of sleepdoctor.com, YouTube, email) makes about $13M of EBITDA before corporate cost on $16M of revenue in 2026. A stand-alone media company of 25 people needs about $2.6M of corporate cost, so about $10.7M of cash a year is harvestable today. Sleep Care loses about $10M a year at the EBITDA line and the shared corporate layer costs another $7.4M; together they consume all of it.

The senior claim is $100.6M at September 2026 and compounds at roughly $10M a year while interest is paid in kind. Management's October ask would extend that arrangement to December 2029 and add a $10M revolver, which means the senior lenders receive no cash until a Sleep Care plan that has already slipped by 14 months reaches breakeven in October 2027.

What holds. Sleep Media still ranks, still monetises at a rising revenue per click, and still generates about $1.3M to $1.5M of cash a month in the holiday quarter. That cash exists and can be captured.

What to watch. Google clicks to sleepfoundation.org fell 48% year on year in October 2026. If that pace continues, the harvest is worth closer to $16M than $29M, which is why the recommendation pairs a harvest with a parallel, reserve-priced sale process.

The PIK period ends this month and the ask extends it three more years.

Cash interest on the senior loan was suspended in May 2025 and the specified PIK period runs to 30 November 2026. On 19 October management asked the lenders to extend the maturity from December 2027 to December 2029, keep interest in kind through maturity, cut minimum liquidity from $2M to $1M and restore a $10M asset-based revolver for insurance working capital.

Under that ask the senior claim grows to about $140M and the subordinated claim to about $125M by December 2029. The company's own base case still runs out of cash in June 2027 without the revolver, and its long-range plan requires Sleep Care revenue to rise from $20M to $56M next year.

What holds. Management has managed cash well against its own forecasts: ending cash beat budget in every month of 2026, helped by $2.1M of domain sales and vendor terms.

What to watch. The December 2026 interest restart is the only moment at which the lenders hold the pen. Agreeing to the extension without a cash sweep gives that moment away for three years.

Sources: Oct-19-26 discussion materials pp.13–18; LRP Covenants-SUMMARY; Nov-25 SteerCo (amendment closed 6 Nov 2025).

Stand-alone, Sleep Media is a $10.7M-a-year cash business, double what management reports.

Management's reporting charges the entire $7.4M corporate layer to Sleep Media, which is why the segment shows $5.6M of EBITDA. The media business itself (25 people, no cost of goods) spends $3.0M a year on content, marketing and tools. A stand-alone owner would add a general manager, a small finance function and audit, legal and insurance, about $2.6M in our base case.

The remaining $4.8M of corporate cost is stranded on Sleep Care in any separation. That is a real cost of the carve-out, but it is Sleep Care's cost, and today it is hidden inside the media segment.

What holds. The margin structure is intact: revenue per click rose from $5.12 in January 2026 to $8.18 in September on the 3Z guaranteed-CPC deal and better page conversion.

What to watch. Any buyer or harvest owner will underwrite 2027 revenue, not 2026; the base case takes it down 12% to $14.3M.

Sources: LRP Financials-SM Less SS, Financials-Shared Serv, Labor; May-26 lender package Consolidating P&L.

Google clicks to sleepfoundation.org have fallen 48% in a year, faster than revenue shows.

Search Console shows 975,000 clicks to sleepfoundation.org in October 2026 against 1.87M in October 2025. Impressions fell 40%, which means the site is being shown less, not just clicked less. Commercial pages were hit unevenly: best-mattress lost 44%, the best-mattress-for-back-pain page lost 83%, while best-pillows gained 179% after the Q2 rebuild.

Revenue has held up because revenue per click and click-through rose and because 3Z guaranteed $16 to $20 a click on 15 pages through December. Those are mitigants with expiry dates, not a reversal of the traffic trend.

What holds. The site still ranks in the top six on average for its impressions, and non-mattress categories (pillows, toppers, trackers) have recovered rank.

What to watch. The 3Z floor expires 31 December 2026; Q1 2027 revenue per click is the first clean read of monetisation without it.

Sources: Google Search Console (Markifact); Q3-26 board preread pp.16–18.

Sleep Care has not earned the media cash; every cohort returns less than it cost.

Short-term value to acquisition cost (STV:CAC, the gross profit a patient generates in four months divided by the cost of acquiring them) was 1.13 in the first quarter of 2026, then fell to 0.89, 0.72 and 0.63 as marketing moved up-funnel and insurance patients converted slowly. The June cohort sits at 0.85 after 80 days; July at 0.62; August at 0.54. Management's own rule is to scale marketing only above 1.0.

September 2026 Sleep Care revenue was $1.25M, the same as September 2025, with the monthly EBITDA loss up from $0.5M to $1.2M. The plan now needs revenue to reach $7.6M a month by December 2027.

What holds. Insurance demand is real: patient forms tripled at 40% of the cost per action once insurance was offered, and the in-house clinic is live.

What to watch. Throughput from insurance form to shipped test was 5.3% in July and about 6% in August against a 16.5% December target; that single number decides whether the 2027 plan is possible.

Sources: Q3-26 board preread; Oct-19-26 discussion materials; Sleep Care dashboard 1 Nov 2026; May-25 Comvest update.

A ring-fenced harvest yields about $29M of cash over five years in the base case.

Stand-alone Sleep Media generates $8.6M of cash in 2027 in the base case (revenue down 12%), falling to $3.6M by 2031, for $29M in total plus a residual sale at three times EBITDA. The management case, with revenue flat to rising, yields $56M of cash and $68M of present value. The severe case, where traffic keeps falling at the current pace, yields only $11M.

Against this, a managed sale of Sleep Media today would likely fetch three to three and a half times stand-alone EBITDA, about $32M to $37M before costs. The harvest and the sale are worth about the same in present value in the base case; the harvest keeps the option to sell later and captures the holiday quarter in hand.

What holds. In every case except severe, the 2027 harvest alone covers 55% to 100% of the cash interest the senior loan would carry at SOFR plus 6.50%.

What to watch. Each 10 points of annual traffic decline removes about $12M of present value; the sale-versus-harvest decision turns on the first two quarters of 2027 traffic.

Sources: CreditSwan model (model.py); LRP; June-26 Comvest sale analysis.

Agree to nothing this month without a cash sweep, spend gates and sponsor money.

The recommendation is a sequenced harvest: first, ring-fence Sleep Media inside the existing credit agreement (separate accounts, cash dominion, a monthly sweep of media free cash to the senior lenders as cash interest) as the price of any PIK or maturity extension. Second, cap Sleep Care at a self-funded budget with three gates, and require Vistria to fund any growth spend above it. Third, prepare the formal carve-out and a reserve-priced sale of Sleep Media in parallel so that the lenders can exit the media asset on the first sign that traffic is not stabilising.

This captures the cash now, keeps the Sleep Care option alive on the sponsor's money rather than the lenders', and avoids selling the media asset in a trough unless a buyer clears the harvest value. Modelled state by state, a sponsor-funded Sleep Care with a lender warrant (Option G) returns more to the senior lenders than the management ask (Option A) whether the plan works, half-works or fails, because the Media cash reaches the lenders in every state instead of being spent first.

What holds. Every element can be documented as an amendment to the existing agreement; the lenders already hold the covenant defaults and the equity pledge that make the ask negotiable.

What to watch. Vistria Credit holds 24% of the senior facility and Vistria owns the equity; required-lender votes should be checked for conflicts before the counter is tabled.

Section 03 · Situation — where OneCare Media stands

A once highly profitable affiliate business now loses money and carries $190M of debt.

This tab sets out the facts the options rest on: five years of results, the record of plans against actuals, the capital structure and covenants, the liquidity runway and the management ask of 19 October 2026. Figures are management's own, taken from the long-range plan, the lender packages and the liquidity certificate.

Revenue peaked at $81M four years ago and the company now runs at a loss.

OneCare Media was bought by Vistria in September 2021 for $336M, or 9.3 times $36M of trailing EBITDA, when sleepfoundation.org held the top organic ranking for best mattress. Google's updates from 2023 onward removed most of that traffic: performance-marketing revenue fell from $78M in 2022 to $23M in 2024 and $17M in 2025. Sleep Care, built from the 2022 Sleep Doctor acquisition, grew from $3M to a forecast $20M but has lost money in every year.

Consolidated reported EBITDA went from $44M in 2021 to a $0.6M loss in 2025 and a forecast $4.2M loss in 2026. Bank EBITDA, the covenant definition that caps add-backs, is a forecast $3.0M loss for 2026 and a $10.9M loss on a trailing basis at September.

What holds. The media business still produces 82% EBITDA margins before corporate cost, and management has cut headcount from 70 at the end of 2023 to 78 today while adding a clinical team.

What to watch. Comvest's 2021 shock case assumed revenue of $20M and EBITDA of $12M; actual 2026 media revenue is $16M and consolidated bank EBITDA is negative.

Sources: Sep-21 IC memo; Apr-24 situation update; LRP Annual P&Ls 25-29 and Covenants-SUMMARY.

Every plan since the 2021 underwriting has missed revenue by at least a quarter.

The 2021 management case had 2026 revenue at $95M; Comvest's own base case had $57M and its downside $38M. The FY24 budget of $58.5M came in at $34.4M. The FY26 budget set in December 2025 called for $50.0M and break-even EBITDA; the forecast is $36.6M and a $4.2M loss. The 2027 figure in the long-range plan has already been cut from $87M in the value-creation plan to $72.5M.

The pattern is consistent: Sleep Media has roughly held its budgets in 2025 and 2026 while Sleep Care has missed by 40% to 50% in each year, and each miss has been followed by a request for more time.

What holds. Management forecasts cash well: the 13-week forecasts have landed within $0.5M of actual in each of the last three cycles, mostly by cutting disbursements when receipts missed.

What to watch. The latest plan asks for Sleep Care to grow 173% in 2027; no prior year has exceeded 40%.

Sources: Sep-21 IC memo pp.90–105; Apr-24 situation update p.9; Q4-25 board pp.43–49; Q3-26 preread p.9; LRP Annual P&Ls.

Total debt has grown to $190M as interest is paid in kind.

At close the structure was a $90M senior term loan and $10M revolver agented by Comvest, a $60M Falcon subordinated note and $95M of sponsor cash equity. Two upsizes in 2022 took the term loan to $104M. Since May 2025 the senior loan has paid interest in kind at SOFR plus 4.50%, and the Falcon note at 12%; both balances compound.

At 30 September 2026 the senior loan stood at $100.6M, the Falcon note at $85.9M and other debt at $3.3M, against $3.2M of cash. Equity is $157M negative on the balance sheet. Under the management ask the senior loan reaches $139.7M and the Falcon note $125.3M by December 2029.

What holds. The senior lenders hold a first lien on substantially all assets and a pledge of the borrower's equity; the Falcon note is subject to a subordination agreement with payment blockage.

What to watch. Vistria's credit fund holds about a quarter of the senior facility, so the required-lender arithmetic and the sponsor's conflict both matter in any vote.

Sources: Sep-21 IC memo pp.7–11; Apr-24 situation update pp.2–7; Oct-19-26 discussion materials p.18; LRP Covenants-SUMMARY.

Liquidity has fallen below $3M and is being bridged by asset sales.

Liquidity under the certificate was $9.1M in October 2024, after the sponsor's $10M equity infusion, and $2.9M at 30 September 2026 against a $2.0M minimum. The 13-week forecast of 2 October has cash at $2.7M on 1 January 2027, of which $1.9M is domain-sale proceeds; excluding those, cash is below $1M from late November.

Testing.com sold for $2.0M in January 2026 and OnlineTherapy.com for about $0.15M in May. The best offer on Telehealth.com, appraised at $1.3M, is $195K. Domains will not bridge another year.

What holds. Debt service is trivial while interest is in kind ($4K over the 13 weeks), so the runway is a function of operating burn alone.

What to watch. Receipts have missed each 13-week forecast on Sleep Care sales; the forecast beats come from deferring vendor and marketing payments, which reverse.

Sources: Liquidity certificate Sep-26; weekly cash forecast 2 Oct 2026; Oct-19-26 discussion materials p.17; Telehealth.com broker thread 21–24 Oct 2026.

The company cannot meet the 2027 minimum-EBITDA tests on its own forecast.

The November 2025 amendment replaced leverage tests with a minimum liquidity test through February 2027 and minimum LTM EBITDA tests from March 2027: $2.0M, $3.5M, $7.0M and $7.5M at successive quarter-ends. The long-range plan forecasts LTM bank EBITDA of ($11.2M) in March 2027, ($7.3M) in June and ($2.3M) in September. The ask moves the first test to March 2028.

The lenders therefore hold a default position in each of the next four quarters whether or not they grant the extension; the question is what to take in exchange for waiving it.

What holds. The documentation already contains the tools: payment blockage on the Falcon note, an automatic block on the management fee, equity cure limits and the equity pledge.

What to watch. Equity cures are limited to five uses and none in consecutive quarters; a sponsor cure of a 2027 EBITDA test would require about $13M, which is the real test of Vistria's intent.

Sources: Oct-19-26 discussion materials p.18; LRP Covenants-SUMMARY; Apr-24 situation update.

The plan behind the ask needs Sleep Care revenue to almost triple in 2027.

The long-range plan has Sleep Care revenue at $55.5M in 2027, $112.5M in 2028 and $158.2M in 2029, with EBITDA of ($3.7M), $15.2M and $35.2M. Insurance revenue goes from $0.9M in 2026 to $18.9M in 2027. The plan's own base case sees cash fall below zero from June 2027 and relies on revolver draws of up to $3.5M, rising to $5.8M in the DME-success case.

Sleep Media is forecast flat at about $17M of revenue and $7M of EBITDA after corporate cost through 2029, which assumes Google traffic stops falling in 2027.

What holds. The plan is internally consistent and the unit-economics bridge is explicit: CAC $132, 10,096 tests a month, STV:CAC 1.85 and $307K of monthly recurring profit at breakeven in October 2027.

What to watch. Today's figures are CAC $265 (August) and about $161 in October after spend cuts, 2,600 to 3,100 tests a month, STV:CAC 0.54 to 0.85 and $94K of recurring profit.

Monthly results show Sleep Media's seasonality and Sleep Care's widening loss.

The monthly series behind the annual figures. Sleep Media revenue is seasonal, peaking in the holiday quarter when mattress sales and guaranteed CPC rates are highest; Sleep Care's loss has widened through 2026 as marketing rose toward $0.9M a month.

What holds. Sleep Media's second half of 2026 is pacing above its first half for the first time since 2023.

What to watch. Sleep Care's September revenue of $1.25M is the lowest month since March 2025.

Section 04 · Sleep Media — the asset to be harvested

Sleep Media earns 82% margins on halved traffic; its value is a race against decline.

This tab describes the asset Comvest would harvest: what it earns, where the revenue comes from, how fast the Google traffic is falling, what it would cost to run on its own, and what it could be sold for. The company's long-range plan assumes the traffic decline stops in 2027; Search Console does not yet show that.

Sleep Foundation search still produces four fifths of media revenue, mostly from mattresses.

Sleep Media revenue was $4.97M in Q3 2025 and is projected at $4.36M for Q3 2026. Google-dependent affiliate revenue remains 80% of the mix; the top three best-mattress pages fell from 34% to 20% of revenue in a year as mid-tier and long-tail mattress pages grew and as advertising, AI referrals, YouTube and email rose from 3% to 20%.

Diversified revenue is real but small: $880K in Q3 2026 across advertising ($358K), YouTube ($222K), AI referrals ($195K) and email ($105K). 3Z Brands alone is 44% of direct advertising, so the non-Google line has its own concentration.

What holds. Management has executed on diversification faster than it planned: non-SEO revenue ran 24% ahead of budget through May and advertising grew 18 times year on year.

What to watch. The 3Z guaranteed-CPC agreement ($16–20 a click on 15 pages) expires 31 December 2026; Q1 2027 revenue per click without it is the first test of the harvest case.

Sources: Q3-26 board preread pp.12–20; Q2-26 preread pp.16–18.

Search Console shows a 48% fall in clicks to sleepfoundation.org over twelve months.

Clicks fell from 1.87M in October 2025 to 975K in October 2026; impressions fell 40% from 173M to 104M, so the site is being shown less often, not merely losing click share to AI answers. Average position improved from 6.3 to 5.8, which is consistent with Google showing fewer results per query rather than ranking the site lower.

Outside evidence points the same way: Chartbeat measured Google referrals to publishers down 40% in the year to July 2026, Ahrefs measured a 58% fall in position-one click-through when an AI Overview appears, and Sistrix listed sleepfoundation.org among the losers of the May 2026 core update at −27% visibility.

What holds. sleepdoctor.com's commercial pages are small ($80K a month) and the clinic buys most of its own traffic, so the media decline does not directly starve Sleep Care.

What to watch. Each successive core update has taken a step down; the next is due in the holiday quarter, when the company earns a third of its media cash.

Sources: Google Search Console; LRP Financials-Sleep Media; Chartbeat (Sep-26); Ahrefs (Feb-26); Sistrix (May-26 core update).

Higher click-through and revenue per click have hidden two years of traffic loss.

Between 2024 and 2026 commercial traffic fell 46% while revenue per visit rose 28%: click-through went from 37% to 47% after the page rebuilds and revenue per click held near $6.50 and then jumped above $8 in the autumn on the 3Z floor. Organic revenue therefore fell 31%, not 46%.

This is good management of a shrinking asset, not growth. The revenue per click gains depend on a handful of mattress partners (3Z, Saatva, Resident) whose own volumes are down: the US mattress industry shipped 13% fewer units in 2025 and Purple is in a strategic review.

What holds. The AI-referral work is measurable: revenue per AI visit rose from $0.35 in January to $1.68 in August, and AI referrals reached 5% of organic revenue.

What to watch. If partners cut commission rates as volumes fall, as publishers reported at the October 2025 Press Gazette event, revenue per click reverses and the decline shows through in full.

Sources: Q3-26 board preread; ISPA 2026 trends report; Press Gazette (Oct-25).

A stand-alone Sleep Media needs about $2.6M of corporate cost, a third of today's charge.

The media business has 25 direct employees in content, marketing, business development, analytics and technology, costing $2.5M in payroll and contractors, plus $0.5M of tools and other costs: $3.0M in total. Management's reporting then charges it the whole shared-services layer: $3.8M of payroll (the executive team, finance, people, analytics), $1.4M of professional fees, $0.8M of software, and $1.4M of facilities, travel, transaction fees and the Vistria management fee.

A stand-alone media company of this size would carry a general manager (the current SVP already runs the unit), a finance lead with outsourced accounting, fractional HR and legal, audit, insurance and its own tool stack. CreditSwan's base estimate is $2.6M; a lean owner could run it at $2.0M.

What holds. None of the direct media cost base has to change; the people who produce the revenue are already in the unit.

What to watch. Whoever owns Sleep Care after a separation inherits about $4.8M of corporate cost that today sits in the media line.

Sources: LRP Financials-Shared Serv, Financials-SM Less SS, Labor, Headcount.

How far the traffic loss runs decides whether revenue halves or holds.

Four revenue paths are modelled from the 2026 forecast of $16.3M. Management's plan grows to $17.5M. The base case takes 12% a year off, consistent with traffic falling 20% and monetisation gaining 8%. The downside takes 20% a year off, the pace of the last two years net of monetisation. The severe case applies the current click decline with no monetisation offset for a year, then eases.

Direct media cost is assumed to flex by a third of the revenue change and stand-alone corporate cost stays at $2.6M, so EBITDA falls faster than revenue in every case except management's.

What holds. Even the downside case produces positive cash for five years; the asset does not go to zero quickly because its cost base is small and partly variable.

What to watch. The severe case runs out of EBITDA in 2031; it is also the case in which a sale in 2027 is clearly better than a harvest.

Sources: CreditSwan model; LRP; Google Search Console.

Comparable publishers trade at about three times EBITDA, which prices Sleep Media in the mid-thirties.

Public search-dependent publishers (Ziff Davis, Future, People Inc.) trade at 3.1 to 3.3 times EBITDA and under one times revenue, with revenue falling mid single digits. Brokered content sites changed hands at about two times annual profit in 2025, with Google-dependent sites discounted further. Lenders took Digital Media Solutions for a $95M credit bid.

Applied to stand-alone EBITDA of $10.7M, a managed process would likely clear at 3.0 to 3.5 times, or $32M to $37M before fees; a forced sale 2.0 to 2.5 times; a strategic buyer with cost synergies (Pillar4, Static Media, Valnet, Ziff Davis) up to 4.5 times if it believes the diversification story. Section 06 · Carve-out — how the separation would work

Separation is a cash-control problem first; most of the value can be ring-fenced in weeks.

This tab answers the mechanical question Comvest asked: can Sleep Media be carved out of Sleep Care and its cash harvested? It maps what moves where, the entanglements between the two businesses, the legal routes and their timing, who has to consent, how the harvest would be run, and the risks that need mitigants.

Sleep Media is domains, an audience and a small team; Sleep Care is a clinic.

The media assets are sleepfoundation.org, sleepapnea.org, the editorial and commercial content on sleepdoctor.com, the YouTube channels, the 3.3M-address email file, the affiliate and advertising contracts, the content management stack and the 25-person media team under the SVP and GM. Sleep Care is the clinic and store on sleepdoctor.com, the professional corporation and MSO, the DME enrolment, the clinical and customer-service staff, inventory, patient records and the Shopify and EHR stack.

Three things straddle the line: the Sleep Doctor brand and Dr. Breus, who front both the media audience and the clinic; the sleepdoctor.com domain, which hosts both; and the corporate layer. Each needs a contract, not a wall.

What holds. The two businesses already keep separate P&Ls, separate marketing budgets and largely separate systems, which is why the lender package can report them side by side.

What to watch. The Dr. Breus agreement, the National Sleep Foundation trademark co-existence terms and the affiliate network contracts should be read for assignment and change-of-control clauses before any entity move.

Sources: LRP Labor and Headcount; Q3-26 preread; Sep-21 IC memo (NSF trademark); Apr-22 Sleep Doctor acquisition release.

The referral path from the media sites to the clinic is small and shrinking.

Sleep Care's gross profit on sales referred from the media sites fell through 2026 and hit its low in August as the best-CPAP pages lost 56% of their ranking value. The referral engine is worth tens of thousands of dollars a month, not millions, and Sleep Care buys most of its own traffic. A separation therefore costs Sleep Care little revenue if a referral agreement is put in place.

In the other direction, Sleep Media uses the corporate layer (finance, people, technology, analytics) and the Dr. Breus audience. Both are solved with a transition services agreement and a brand licence.

What holds. A commercial referral agreement (affiliate terms at market rates) formalises what already happens and gives MediaCo a new customer.

What to watch. The OSA screener on sleepapnea.org and the insurance-referred tests are the referral paths with growth; they should be priced into the agreement rather than given away.

Sources: Q2-26 and Q3-26 prereads (SC gross profit contribution); CreditSwan.

Five legal routes exist; the amendment ring-fence is fastest and the prepack is surest.

The lightest route is an amendment inside the existing credit agreement: separate bank accounts for the media business, cash dominion, a budget for Sleep Care and a monthly sweep of media free cash to the lenders. It needs required-lender approval and the borrower's signature, takes weeks and changes no titles. The next step is to drop the media assets into a new restricted subsidiary that joins as guarantor and grantor, which makes Falcon structurally subordinate to MediaCo-level claims.

If the sponsor refuses, the lenders can enforce: a strict foreclosure or Article 9 sale of the pledged equity or assets takes weeks to months but carries fraudulent-transfer risk when juniors are out of the money; a prepackaged Chapter 11 with a 363 sale takes one to six months, costs more and binds holdouts. The precedents that split a business (Vice, Digital Media Solutions, Anthology, Instant Brands) all used Chapter 11.

What holds. Every route starts the same way: the lenders set the terms of the December 2026 amendment, and the sponsor decides whether to sign.

What to watch. No clean out-of-court precedent exists in which lenders took the cash division and the sponsor kept the growth division; expect Falcon and Vistria to require court cover or payment for a consensual split.

Sources: Research brief (restructuring precedents) 3 Nov 2026; law-firm client alerts listed on the Assumptions tab.

Comvest controls the required-lender vote, but Vistria sits on both sides of the table.

Comvest funds hold about 62% of the senior facility and act as agent; Vistria Credit holds 24%, Cliffwater 8% and Pantheon 6%. Required-lender actions pass with Comvest alone; sacred rights (rate reductions, maturity extensions for all lenders, release of all or substantially all collateral) typically need affected lenders. Vistria's equity controls the board and the borrower's signature; its credit fund votes on the senior side.

Falcon's $86M subordinated note is blocked from cash payments after a covenant default and is subject to a standstill; it cannot block an enforcement but can object to a strict foreclosure's valuation and could petition an involuntary case. Falcon received no value in a sale below about $103M and has every incentive to keep the company whole and PIKing.

What holds. The senior lenders already hold Class L units in the sponsor blocker from the November 2025 amendment, a precedent for equity consideration in exchange for accommodation.

What to watch. Check the subordination agreement's senior-debt cap: three years of PIK may have consumed the headroom that makes the extended senior claim 'senior debt' for Falcon's purposes.

Sources: Apr-24 situation update; Oct-19-26 materials; Comvest BDC 10-Q 30 Jun 2026; Bass Berry (intercreditor terms).

The sweep should be monthly, budget-based and paid as cash interest.

MediaCo (or the ring-fenced media accounts) operates to an approved annual budget of direct costs plus its stand-alone corporate charge. Each month, cash above a $1.0M operating reserve is swept to the agent and applied first as cash interest on the senior loan and then to principal. Sleep Care's budget is approved separately and funded only from its own receipts, the remaining cash and sponsor money; no media cash crosses the line without lender consent.

Over 2027 to 2031 the base case sweeps about $29M; the management case about $57M. The sweep also makes the sale option real: a buyer sees audited stand-alone accounts and a clean perimeter.

What holds. The company already produces a weekly 13-week cash forecast and a monthly segment P&L; the sweep adds an account structure and a certificate, not a new finance function.

What to watch. Tax distributions to members on partnership income are the one leakage to control: media income allocated to the blocker generates a tax distribution right that should be subordinated to the sweep.

Sources: CreditSwan design; tax and legal references on the Assumptions tab.

The two risks that matter are the next Google update and the sponsor walking away.

A harvest is a bet that the media asset declines slowly enough to be worth more held than sold. The next core update is the main risk to that bet, and the reserve-priced sale process is the hedge. The second risk is that Vistria refuses the ring-fence and lets the company default, forcing an enforcement route that costs time and money and could lose the management team.

The stranded corporate cost is a cost of separation, not of the harvest: it exists today, hidden in the media line, and it falls on whoever owns Sleep Care.

What holds. The media team is small, remote and has a clear leader; retention is a matter of a modest plan.

What to watch. Marketing spend in Q4 is the fastest way the holiday cash leaves the building; the ring-fence has to be in place before Black Friday.
